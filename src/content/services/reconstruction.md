When a burst pipe, house fire, or wind event tears into the structure of a home, the demolition and drying crews eventually leave, but the hole in the wall, the missing subfloor, and the exposed framing don't fix themselves. That gap between "the damage is stopped" and "the house is whole again" is where reconstruction services in Abilene come in, turning a gutted kitchen, a fire-damaged roofline, or a water-soaked bathroom back into a space that passes inspection and feels like home again.

## What reconstruction services actually involves

Post-damage reconstruction picks up where mitigation ends. It covers everything from replacing a single section of drywall and flooring after a slow leak to full structural rebuilds after a house fire, including framing, roofing, electrical and plumbing rough-in, insulation, drywall, paint, flooring, and trim. Abilene's housing stock runs from older brick-and-frame homes near downtown to newer slab-on-grade construction in the surrounding subdivisions, and the rebuild approach differs depending on foundation type, wall assembly, and what the original structure can and can't support after fire or water exposure. Timelines vary widely: a single room with cosmetic drywall and flooring repair might run one to two weeks, while a structural rebuild after a fire or major storm event can take several months once permitting, framing inspections, and finish work are factored in.

## Our process

1. **Scope of work and documentation.** Every reconstruction project starts with a written scope that itemizes what was removed during mitigation, what structural elements were affected, and what needs to be replaced versus repaired. This document becomes the basis for both the work plan and the insurance claim.
2. **Permits and code review.** Structural work, electrical, and plumbing changes typically require permits through the City of Abilene or the relevant county authority. We identify what triggers a permit before framing starts, not after an inspector flags it.
3. **Framing and rough-in.** Damaged studs, joists, and subfloor are replaced to match existing structure where possible. Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC rough-in happens before insulation and drywall go back, and rough-in work gets inspected before it's covered.
4. **Insulation, drywall, and finish work.** Walls are closed, textured to match surrounding surfaces, and painted. Flooring, trim, cabinetry, and fixtures go back in during this phase.
5. **Final walkthrough and punch list.** Before the job closes out, we walk the space with the homeowner or property manager to confirm the finish work matches expectations and that any permit-required inspections have passed.

## What separates a good reconstruction response from a bad one

The most common mistake in post-disaster rebuilding is skipping ahead to finish work before the structure underneath is actually sound. Drywall and paint over a subfloor that's still slightly elevated in moisture content, or over framing that wasn't properly sistered after fire damage, looks fine for a few months and then shows up as cupped flooring, cracked drywall seams, or a musty smell that reopens the whole claim. A careful reconstruction crew verifies moisture content in framing and subfloor before closing walls, pulls permits for structural and electrical work instead of hoping an inspector doesn't ask, and matches existing materials (brick, siding, trim profiles) closely enough that repairs don't stand out. Insurance adjusters reviewing a reconstruction claim typically want to see that the scope of work ties back to the original mitigation report, that permitted work has inspection sign-off, and that materials and labor are itemized rather than lumped into a single estimate line.

## What does reconstruction services cost?

Reconstruction costs depend heavily on the extent of structural damage, the size of the affected area, and whether permits and inspections are required. These are typical industry ranges, not a quote: every loss is different, and Air Care Restoration provides a written scope of work before any reconstruction begins so you know what's included.

| Scenario | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Single room drywall and flooring repair | $1,500 - $4,500 |
| Bathroom or kitchen rebuild after water damage | $5,000 - $15,000 |
| Fire damage reconstruction, single room with structural framing | $10,000 - $30,000 |
| Partial structural rebuild (multi-room, framing and rough-in) | $25,000 - $75,000 |
| Full structural reconstruction after major fire or storm loss | $75,000 and up |

Homeowners insurance typically covers reconstruction costs tied to a covered peril, such as fire, a sudden pipe burst, or wind damage, though coverage for code upgrades and permit costs varies by policy, so it's worth confirming those line items with your adjuster early.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

West Texas summers bring extreme heat that can affect curing times for drywall mud, paint, and exterior materials, so finish schedules sometimes shift to early morning work during peak months. Winter freeze events, like the kind that hit the region periodically, are also a common trigger for reconstruction work after burst pipes damage framing, flooring, and drywall in homes that weren't built with the same pipe insulation standards found further north. Storm season can bring wind and hail damage that requires roof and siding reconstruction alongside interior repairs.
