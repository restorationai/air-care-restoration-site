## What Trauma Scene Cleanup actually involves

Trauma scene cleaning is biohazard remediation first and emotional support second. Technicians work in full PPE to remove and dispose of affected materials, which sometimes includes carpet, subflooring, drywall, or upholstery that can't be salvaged once bodily fluids have penetrated porous surfaces. Hard surfaces are cleaned with hospital-grade disinfectants, and the air space is treated separately for odor. Timelines vary with the size of the area and how long the material sat before discovery; a contained incident on tile might be finished same-day, while a scene that went unnoticed for days can require subfloor removal and extended air treatment.

## Our process

1. **Scene assessment and containment.** We identify every surface and material affected, set up containment barriers, and confirm scope with the property owner or family representative before anything is touched.
2. **PPE staging and controlled entry.** Technicians suit up according to OSHA bloodborne pathogen protocols and limit entry to trained crew only, keeping the rest of the property undisturbed.
3. **Removal of affected materials.** Non-salvageable porous materials (carpet, padding, sections of drywall or subfloor) are removed and bagged as biohazard waste per state handling requirements.
4. **Decontamination and enzymatic treatment.** Remaining surfaces are cleaned, disinfected, and treated with enzymatic solutions that break down residual organic material rather than just masking it.
5. **Deodorization and final verification.** We treat the air and any affected structural cavities for odor, then walk the space with you or your point of contact to confirm it's ready to re-enter.

## What separates a good trauma scene cleanup from a bad one

The most common failure is treating this as a surface cleaning job. Bodily fluids wick into grout lines, subfloor seams, and the underside of baseboards; a crew that only wipes visible surfaces leaves contamination and odor behind that surfaces again within days. Another mistake is improper waste handling, biohazard material has to be bagged, labeled, and disposed of according to state regulations, not thrown in a household bin. Insurance adjusters reviewing these claims look for documented chain of custody on waste disposal, itemized material removal, and before-and-after photos that justify what was torn out versus what was cleaned in place. A disciplined crew documents all of it as they go, which also protects the family from disputes over what was actually necessary.
