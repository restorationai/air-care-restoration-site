## Fire Department, Restoration Crew, or Insurance: Who Do You Call First?

Call 911 first if there is any active fire, a gas smell, sparking wiring, or smoke still visible, before anyone calls a restoration company. Once the fire department has cleared the structure as safe to re-enter, that is the moment to call a smoke damage crew, not before and not days later.

A plumber or electrician only enters the picture if the fire damaged a specific water line or circuit the fire department flags during walkthrough. Your insurance carrier gets called the same day too, but after you have a restoration company on record documenting the loss. Adjusters move faster and pay more accurately when there is a written scope of damage in front of them instead of a verbal description.

## Smoke Damage vs. Fire Damage: Who Handles Which?

Fire damage means charred, burned, or structurally compromised material; smoke damage means the residue, staining, and odor that travels through the rest of the house even where flames never reached. Both usually need the same crew, because one almost always comes with the other.

A single kitchen fire can leave char on the cabinets above the stove (fire damage) while coating every soft surface in the living room, hallway closets, and HVAC ductwork with soot film and odor (smoke damage). Separating the two into different contractors usually means duplicated work and gaps in the insurance claim, so most Abilene homeowners have one crew handle both under a single scope. ## Why Does Soot Get Worse the Longer It Sits?

Soot is mildly acidic and starts etching metal, grout, and painted surfaces within hours, which is why same-day cleanup matters more than most people expect. Chrome fixtures tarnish, white grout lines yellow permanently, and plastic surfaces can pit if soot is left untreated through a hot West Texas afternoon.

Dry soot from a fast-burning fire (paper, wood) wipes away more easily than wet, smoldering soot from synthetics (carpet, foam, plastics), which smears and bonds to surfaces on contact. Professional cleanup follows the [ANSI/IICRC S700 standard](https://iicrc.org/s700/) for fire and smoke restoration, which spells out the correct cleaning agent and technique for each soot type rather than one all-purpose spray. A deeper walkthrough of that process is in [fire/soot restoration: what to expect](/blog/fire-soot-restoration/).

## What Happens Before Anyone Arrives (The First 24 Hours)?

Do not run the HVAC system, do not wipe soot with a dry cloth, and do not eat anything that was exposed to smoke, even in sealed containers. Running the furnace or air conditioner spreads soot through every supply register in the house; a dry wipe grinds soot particles deeper into fabric and drywall instead of lifting them.

Open windows if it's safe and the weather allows it, photograph every room before touching anything, and keep receipts for anything you buy in the interim (hotel, clothing, meals). Those photos and receipts are what your insurance claim leans on later. If smoke alarms, wiring, or the roof structure look compromised, do not re-enter until the fire department or a structural contractor signs off.

## What Does an Abilene Smoke Damage Crew Do on the First Visit?

The first visit is an inspection and containment setup, not a full cleanup: expect moisture and air-quality readings, a room-by-room damage walkthrough, and HEPA air scrubbers placed to stop soot particles from migrating further through the house. The crew also photographs and documents everything for the insurance file before any cleaning starts.

From there, the scope typically separates into structural cleaning (walls, ceilings, HVAC ducts), contents cleaning (furniture, textiles, personal items), and odor treatment, since soot smell lingers in porous materials long after visible residue is gone. That last piece often runs through a dedicated [odor removal](/services/odor-removal/) process involving thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators rather than air freshener.

## When Is It Not Safe to Stay in the House?

A house is not safe to stay in if smoke alarms are disabled, the HVAC system was running during the fire, soot has reached bedrooms or areas where people sleep, or there is any standing water from firefighting efforts mixing with electrical systems. Any of those conditions means temporary relocation while crews work, not just an inconvenience to tolerate.

Smoke odor alone in a room you're not using daily isn't necessarily a safety issue, but it is a sign that cleanup needs to start soon before it bonds further into carpet, drywall, and insulation.
