## How to Compare Mold Remediation Companies Before You Hire

Before you sign anything, run through this short checklist. It takes five minutes and can save you from a contractor who is not legally authorized to do the work.

**1. Verify the Texas mold license.** Go to [tdlr.texas.gov](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/mold/mold.htm) and search the contractor's name or license number. If they cannot provide a license number, they cannot legally perform mold remediation in Texas.

**2. Ask for a written scope of work.** A legitimate remediation contractor will give you a written protocol before starting. This document describes the containment strategy, the removal method, the disposal plan, and the clearance testing standard. No written scope means no accountability.

**3. Confirm they do clearance testing, or explain who does.** In Texas, the mold assessment and the mold remediation are legally required to be performed by separate parties. A remediation contractor cannot certify their own work as clear. Ask who will perform the post-remediation clearance test.

**4. Check for IICRC certification.** The [IICRC's remediation standard (IICRC S520)](https://www.iicrc.org/standards) defines the protocol for mold remediation. Firms that follow it use containment barriers, negative air pressure, HEPA vacuuming, and documented drying verification. Ask if they follow S520.

**5. Confirm insurance coverage.** General liability and workers' compensation protect you if something goes wrong on your property. Ask for a certificate of insurance before work starts.

## What Mold Remediation Actually Involves

Mold remediation is not mold removal in the sense of wiping a surface and calling it done. Professional remediation follows a defined process:

1. **Containment**, the affected area is sealed with poly sheeting and placed under negative air pressure so spores cannot migrate to clean areas during removal.
2. **PPE and worker protection**, technicians work in full Tyvek suits with N95 or P100 respirators and face shields. This is not optional.
3. **Removal of affected materials**, porous materials (drywall, insulation, wood framing) that cannot be cleaned to clearance standards are removed and double-bagged for disposal.
4. **HEPA vacuuming and surface cleaning**, remaining surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent.
5. **Drying verification**, if a moisture source caused the mold, the moisture problem must be resolved and the area dried to acceptable levels before reconstruction. Skipping this step guarantees the mold returns.
6. **Clearance testing**, a separate, licensed mold assessment consultant (not the remediation contractor) collects air or surface samples and verifies the area meets clearance standards.

If a company skips any of these steps, the job is not done correctly. For more on what mold testing looks like before remediation begins, see: [How To Test for Mold in Your Home (DIY Kits vs a Pro Inspection)](/blog/how-to-test-for-mold/).
