## Do You Call a Roofer or a Restoration Company First?

Call a restoration company first if water is getting inside the house right now; call a roofer first if the roof deck held and you only need an estimate for shingle or decking repair. In West Texas hail season, most storm calls need both: temporary protection and drying immediately, then a permanent roof repair on the roofer's own schedule.

Air Care's crews handle the parts of storm recovery that can't wait on a roofer: covering the opening with an [emergency tarp](/services/emergency-board-up-tarping/), boarding up a blown-out window, and pulling standing water before it soaks into drywall, insulation, and subfloor. A full roof replacement or shingle tear-off is a roofer's job, not a restoration company's, and Air Care will say so rather than take on work outside its scope.

If a power line is down, you smell gas, or the structure looks unsafe to enter, call 911 or your utility provider first. Tarping and drying wait until the scene is safe. Once the hazard is cleared, call Air Care for the tarp, board-up, and [water extraction](/services/water-damage-restoration/), and call the insurance company once mitigation is already underway, not before. Most policies require the homeowner to prevent further damage, and a tarped roof is part of meeting that duty. ## What Should You Photograph Before Cleanup Starts?

Photograph the roof damage, interior water stains, and any broken windows or siding before anyone starts tarping or drying, because insurers want to see the damage in its original state. Time-stamped photos from several angles protect the claim far more than a description after the fact.

Shoot the roof from the ground (zoomed in on missing shingles or hail dents), the gutters and any damaged siding or AC condenser fins, interior ceiling stains, and the water line on any standing water before it's pumped out. Keep a piece of damaged shingle or trim if you can. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/settling-insurance-claims-after-a-disaster) notes that photo documentation and an itemized list of damage are two of the biggest factors in a fast disaster claim settlement. The [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/disasters.html) gives Texas homeowners the same advice: document first, then start drying.

## How Fast Does Wet Drywall and Insulation Need to Come Out?

Wet drywall and insulation need to come out within 24 to 48 hours in most cases, because mold can begin growing on saturated material in that window. Waiting longer turns a water problem into a mold problem that costs more to fix.

Insulation rarely dries in place once it's soaked; most of it gets pulled and replaced. Drywall sometimes dries if the water was clean and caught fast, but once it's been wet more than a day it usually comes out too. Air Care's technicians hold the IICRC Water Restoration Technician (WRT) credential and dry structures to the [ANSI/IICRC S500](https://iicrc.org/s500/) standard, documenting moisture readings at every stage for the adjuster.
