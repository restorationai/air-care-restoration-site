## What training and certification should a biohazard cleanup company actually have?

At minimum, every technician handling blood or bodily fluids needs OSHA bloodborne pathogen training under [29 CFR 1910.1030](https://www.osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens), and the company should be able to tell you that without hesitating. That's the federal floor, not a bonus credential.

Above that floor sits the [IICRC's ANSI/IICRC S540 Standard for Trauma and Crime Scene Cleanup](https://iicrc.org/s540/), which sets the industry process for how a scene gets assessed, contained, cleaned, and verified clean. A company whose technicians hold the IICRC Trauma and Crime Scene Technician (TCST) credential has been trained specifically for this work, not assigned to it because they happened to be free that day.

Air Care Restoration's crews carry the IICRC Trauma and Crime Scene Technician (TCST) and Hazardous Substance Technician (HST) certifications, on top of the company's status as an [IICRC Certified Firm](https://www.iicrc.org/). Those credentials, not a general cleaning background, are what should show up when you ask who's actually coming to the property.

## What questions should you ask before hiring a biohazard cleanup crew?

Ask these four before you sign anything: who removes the contaminated materials and how, whether they document the job for your insurance adjuster, whether they bill insurance directly, and how they confirm the space is actually clean before they leave.

1. **Who physically removes contaminated materials, and how is it handled afterward?** Texas regulates the handling and transport of contaminated waste, and a legitimate company should have a clear answer here, not a shrug.
2. **Will they document the job with photos and a written scope?** This is the paperwork your insurance adjuster will ask for. If a company can't describe how they document a job, that's a red flag.
3. **Can they bill your insurance directly, or do you pay out of pocket first?** Direct billing matters a lot when you're already dealing with a loss, financial and otherwise.
4. **How do they confirm the space is clean before handing it back?** A visual and odor check is the minimum; ATP testing or another documented clearance method is better, and you should be able to see the result.

Many homeowners and renters policies include coverage for trauma or crime scene cleanup following a covered event, though the exact trigger, limits, and language vary by carrier and policy, as the [Insurance Information Institute explains](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance). Check your specific policy, or ask the restoration company to help you find that language.

Call your insurer as early as possible and keep any police report or other official documentation of the incident, since most carriers will ask for it.
