## Why Hamlin Properties See Storm Damage

Hamlin sits on the open plains along the Highway 83 corridor, where there's little tree cover or terrain to slow a fast-moving front. Spring and early summer bring supercell thunderstorms capable of golf-ball hail and straight-line gusts over 70 mph, and the flat farmland surrounding town gives tornadoes a clear runway with almost no warning buffer. A lot of the housing stock here is older frame construction with composition shingle roofs installed decades ago, plus outbuildings and barns with metal roofing that can peel back in a single gust. Once a roof is breached, West Texas heat does the rest: attic temperatures climbing past 120 degrees can cook wet insulation and framing into a mold problem within 48 to 72 hours if the water isn't extracted and the space isn't dried properly.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Hamlin

We start with a roof and exterior assessment to find every point of entry, not just the obvious one, since wind-driven rain can travel sideways under shingles and emerge several feet from where it entered. Emergency tarping and board-up go up first to stop further loss, followed by water extraction from attic insulation, ceiling cavities, and any flooring below a compromised roof line. Downed tree limbs and debris get cleared so our crew and equipment can actually reach the structure, which matters on the rural and semi-rural lots common around Hamlin where driveways are long and storm debris tends to scatter across the whole property. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with moisture readings logged in wall cavities and subfloor until the structure reads dry, not just dry to the touch. Reconstruction, when needed, is scheduled once the insurance scope is approved.

## Hamlin Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most storm claims here involve wind and hail endorsements rather than flood policies, and adjusters covering this part of Jones County are often working several towns in a single day after a regional storm system. We photograph and document damage room by room and shingle by shingle where relevant, which keeps the claim moving even if the adjuster's own site visit gets delayed by a backlog. Texas law gives property owners specific windows for filing supplemental claims if hidden damage turns up after the first inspection, something that happens often with hail, since cracked underlayment or bruised shingles don't always show until a slow leak develops weeks later.

## Local note

One thing crews learn working storms in this part of Texas: mesquite and caliche soil don't drain the way clay or loam does elsewhere in the state. After a hard rain follows wind damage, water can pool against a foundation for days instead of soaking in, which means a roof leak and a rising grade problem sometimes show up at the same time. We check foundation moisture alongside roof and attic damage on every storm call out here, because treating them as separate issues can mean missing one of them entirely.
