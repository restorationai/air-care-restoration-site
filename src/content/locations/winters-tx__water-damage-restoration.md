## Why Winters Properties See Water Damage Issues

Slab-on-grade construction is the norm across Runnels County, and that means water from a failed water heater, a cracked supply line, or a sewer backup often has nowhere to go but laterally, under cabinetry and through wall cavities, before anyone notices a puddle. Older homes in town, many built mid-century, still have galvanized or early copper plumbing that's reaching the end of its service life, and pinhole leaks inside walls can run for days before staining shows. Flash flooding from fast-moving West Texas thunderstorms is another factor: a hard rain event can overwhelm drainage around properties that were never graded for heavy runoff, pushing water in through door thresholds and low-set additions.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Winters

We start with extraction using truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much standing water is involved, then move to moisture mapping with penetrating and non-penetrating meters so we know exactly how far the water traveled under flooring and behind baseboards. On slab homes, that often means checking moisture levels at multiple points across a room rather than assuming the wet area is contained to where the water was visible. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed to pull moisture out of slab-adjacent framing and subfloor materials that can hold water longer than people expect. We document every reading throughout the dry-out so there's a clear record for your insurance carrier and so we know when materials have actually reached dry standard, not just dry to the touch.
