## Why Stamford Properties See Water Damage Fast

Much of Stamford's housing stock dates from the mid-20th century, built on slab foundations that were standard practice across Jones County at the time. Slab construction means a water intrusion, whether from a failed water heater, a cracked supply line, or a roof leak during a hard rain, has nowhere to drain on its own. Water spreads laterally under baseboards and into wall cavities instead of pooling visibly, which is why homeowners often underestimate how much moisture is actually present until flooring starts to cup or drywall starts to bubble. The region's clay-heavy soil also plays a role: after heavy rain, that soil swells and can push groundwater toward foundations that weren't sealed with today's vapor barriers, adding a slow-seep problem on top of whatever caused the original leak.

West Texas thunderstorms are another factor. When they hit Jones County, they tend to dump a lot of rain in a short window, and Stamford's older stormwater infrastructure can get overwhelmed faster than it would in a city with more modern drainage. ## Our Emergency Water Removal Process in Stamford

The first step on arrival is always assessment: how far has the water traveled, is it clean, gray, or contaminated, and what's underneath the visible surface. In slab-foundation homes, we check moisture readings along baseboards and into adjoining rooms before we even start pulling water, because the real damage is usually wider than the wet carpet or buckled laminate suggests. Extraction comes next, using truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on volume and access, followed by setting air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's size and the material types involved. Older homes with plaster or lath-and-plaster sections, still found in some of Stamford's original housing stock, hold moisture longer than drywall and need adjusted drying timelines rather than a one-size approach. We monitor and document moisture levels daily, drying to the IICRC S500 standard rather than guessing at when a space is dry enough to close out.
