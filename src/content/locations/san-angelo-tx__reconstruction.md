Rebuilding after a fire, storm, or major water loss in San Angelo means more than framing and drywall. It means accounting for West Texas clay soils that shift with every dry spell and flash flood, older masonry and brick homes near downtown and the Concho River corridor, and a building season that runs hot enough to change how materials cure. Air Care Restoration handles the structural rebuild from the point your insurance adjuster signs off through final walkthrough, working as a licensed Texas contractor on the reconstruction side of the loss.

## Why San Angelo Properties See Reconstruction Issues

Tom Green County sits on expansive clay that swells when it rains and contracts hard during the long summer dry stretch. That movement stresses slab foundations and the framing above them, and it is a major reason fire and water losses here often turn into full structural rebuilds rather than simple patch jobs. Hailstorms that roll through the region periodically crack roof decking and siding on homes built decades apart, from the brick ranch stock near the Concho River to newer construction further out. Older homes in the city's established neighborhoods also tend to carry original rock or brick veneer that is harder to match once a section has to come down, which changes both the timeline and the materials sourcing for the rebuild.

## Our Reconstruction Services Process in San Angelo

Once demolition and drying are complete, our crews document every removed structural element with photos and measurements before framing begins, which matters for both the permit inspection and the insurance file. We rebuild to match existing roof pitch, wall assemblies, and exterior finishes wherever the original material can be sourced or reasonably approximated, and we coordinate permitting through the city's building department for any load-bearing or electrical work. On slab-on-grade homes common across San Angelo, we check for movement in the foundation before closing up walls, since rebuilding over a slab that is still settling after a fire or flood often leads to callbacks within the first year.

## Reaching San Angelo from Abilene

We run crews out to San Angelo from our Abilene base along US-277 and US-87, a route our teams cover regularly enough that staging equipment and ordering materials for a multi-week rebuild does not add the delays a one-off out-of-town contractor would run into. Because reconstruction is scheduled work rather than an emergency callout, we plan site visits and crew time around your adjuster's timeline and your own schedule rather than treating it as a rush job.

## San Angelo Insurance Coordination

Most reconstruction work here follows a fire, storm, or water claim, so we build the scope of repair directly off the adjuster's estimate and keep documentation, photos, and material invoices organized for the claim file. When the rebuild scope grows once walls are opened, which happens more often on older San Angelo homes than newer construction, we flag it early with supplemental photos so the carrier can review the change before work stalls.

## Local note

Homes close to the Concho River and in the city's older core sit on soil that moves more than people expect between a wet spring and a dry August. We have learned to let a damaged slab sit through at least one weather swing before we commit to final framing measurements, because closing in walls too early on a foundation that is still settling after a loss is one of the more common reasons San Angelo rebuilds need rework later. It costs a little patience up front and saves a callback down the line.
