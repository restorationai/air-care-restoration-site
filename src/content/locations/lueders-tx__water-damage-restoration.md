## Why Lueders Properties See Water Damage Issues

Lueders grew up around its limestone quarries, and a good share of the town's housing stock reflects that: rock and masonry construction, pier-and-beam foundations, and plumbing that in many cases predates the 1970s. Pier-and-beam crawlspaces trap moisture differently than a slab, water pools under the subfloor and can sit there for days without a visible sign inside the house, while the joists and subfloor above slowly take on moisture. Add in Jones County's clay-heavy caliche soil, which swells when wet and drains slowly, and a yard that floods after heavy rain can push water right up against a foundation that was never sealed to modern standards. Many properties here also run on private wells or older rural water lines, so a failed pressure tank or a split supply line can dump water into a crawlspace for hours before anyone notices the pressure drop.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lueders

We start by confirming the water source and shutting it off, which on well-fed properties sometimes means working with the pressure system rather than just a municipal shutoff valve. From there we extract standing water, pull back flooring or baseboards where needed to check for hidden saturation in subfloor framing, and set containment to protect dry areas of the home. Drying is where pier-and-beam and masonry homes behave differently than newer slab construction: a stone or limestone wall releases moisture slowly, so we monitor with moisture meters over several days rather than pulling equipment the moment surfaces feel dry. We document everything as we go, which matters if a claim is being filed against a homeowner's or farm policy.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of homeowners off guard: Lueders' native limestone, the same stone quarried locally for generations of Texas buildings, is porous enough that it can wick water upward from a wet foundation line into interior walls. On a rock or limestone-faced home, that means drying has to account for the wall assembly itself, not just the drywall or baseboard in front of it, or moisture gets trapped behind the surface and shows up as efflorescence or a musty smell weeks later.
