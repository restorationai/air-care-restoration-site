## Why Cisco Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Eastland County gets hit by two distinct storm patterns: spring and early summer supercells that bring large hail and tornado-warned rotation, and summer downburst winds that snap tree limbs and tear loose roofing on older homes. A lot of Cisco's housing stock dates to the mid-1900s oil-boom era, with low-slope composition roofs and original wood fascia that weren't built for repeated hail impact. Metal outbuildings and barns common on the area's ranch and farm properties also take direct hits, and a dented metal roof can hide a puncture that leaks for weeks before it's noticed. Add in overhead utility lines that run along rural county roads outside the city limits, and a single storm can mean downed power along with roof and window damage, which complicates drying equipment setup until power is restored.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Cisco

We start with emergency board-up and tarping to stop active water intrusion, since an open roof section in this part of Texas can take on hundreds of gallons during a slow-moving thunderstorm. From there we extract standing water from attics, ceilings, and interior rooms, pull wet insulation before it compresses and loses R-value, and set drying equipment calibrated to the humidity swings common here, where a hot, dry afternoon can follow a soaking storm within hours and mask how wet the structure still is underneath. Once the structure is stabilized, our crews document every affected material with photos for the claim file and move into reconstruction, whether that's a full roof section, drywall and insulation, or flooring that sat under standing water. Tree and debris removal from the loss area is coordinated as part of the same job so a felled limb or shredded fence line doesn't sit on top of an active repair.
