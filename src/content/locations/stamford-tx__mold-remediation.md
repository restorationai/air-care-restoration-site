**Finding mold in a Stamford home usually surprises people, because West Texas is supposed to be too dry for it.** The truth is more complicated. Between swamp coolers running all summer, slab foundations that shift with the caliche clay under Jones County, and older homes built before attic ventilation codes tightened up, Stamford properties get pockets of trapped humidity that a dry climate alone won't fix. If you're seeing dark staining on drywall, smelling something musty in a closet or bathroom, or just found a slow leak that's been running longer than anyone noticed, that's worth a real look before it spreads further into framing and insulation.

## Why Stamford Properties See Mold Issues

Most of the mold calls we see out this way trace back to one of three things. First, evaporative coolers, still common across Jones County because they're cheap to run in low-humidity heat, push moist air into attics and wall cavities that were never built to handle it, especially in homes from the cotton and ranching boom era with original ductwork. Second, slab foundations here move with the clay-heavy soil through wet and dry cycles, and a hairline crack under a bathroom or kitchen slab can leak for months before anyone sees a stain. Third, Stamford doesn't get much rain, but when it comes it tends to come hard and fast, and flat or low-slope roofs on older commercial buildings and some ranch-style homes don't always shed that volume fast enough, so water finds its way into soffits and ceiling cavities. None of these are dramatic floods. They're slow, quiet sources, which is exactly why the mold has time to establish before anyone calls.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Stamford

We start by finding the moisture source, not just the visible growth, because treating the mold without fixing what's feeding it means you're paying for the same job twice. That usually means checking the attic above any evaporative cooler penetration, moisture-mapping slab perimeters in bathrooms and kitchens, and inspecting roof penetrations on flat sections. Once we've confirmed the scope, we isolate the affected area with physical containment and negative air pressure so spores don't travel into the rest of the house while work is underway. Affected material gets removed and disposed of properly, surfaces are treated and HEPA-vacuumed, and air scrubbers run until conditions meet the clearance standard we're working toward. Our crews operate under Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation mold remediation licensing, and the whole process follows the same documentation and containment protocols used industry-wide, so there's a clear record if your insurance carrier needs one.
