**Lead paint abatement in San Angelo** carries its own timeline pressure: the city's older housing stock, much of it built well before the 1978 federal lead paint ban, means a routine remodel or window replacement can turn into a regulated abatement project the moment a contractor disturbs a painted surface. If you're staring down a renovation permit, a home inspection flag, or a tenant complaint involving peeling paint, the clock and the paperwork both start moving fast.

## Why San Angelo Properties See Lead Paint Issues

San Angelo's housing stock spans several building booms, and a meaningful share of it predates 1978, which means lead-based paint is still present under newer coats in a lot of these homes. Add in the dry, dusty conditions typical of this part of West Texas, and painted trim, siding, and window sashes tend to chalk and crack faster than they would in a wetter climate. That chalking paint sheds lead dust around foundations and window wells, which is exactly the pathway the EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting rule was written to address. Tom Green County permitting offices are seeing more renovation and remodel applications on older homes, and any project that disturbs six square feet of interior paint or twenty square feet of exterior paint on a pre-1978 property falls under RRP work practice requirements.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in San Angelo

We start with testing, not assumptions. A homeowner who thinks "it's probably fine, the house was repainted in the 90s" is often surprised to learn lead paint is layered under three or four coats applied since. Our crews pull samples from trim, sills, and any surface scheduled for sanding, scraping, or demolition, and we document results before any abatement work begins. Once lead is confirmed, we seal the work area with containment barriers, use HEPA-filtered equipment to control dust, and follow EPA Lead-Safe work practices through removal, cleanup, and clearance verification. For occupied homes, that containment matters more in San Angelo than in milder climates: HVAC systems run harder and longer here, and a poorly sealed containment zone lets conditioned air (and dust) migrate through the house faster than it would in a shoulder-season climate.

## Local note

One thing we watch closely on San Angelo jobs: older homes here were frequently repainted with oil-based exterior paint through the 1970s and 80s, and that paint tends to crack in long, vertical sheets under West Texas sun exposure rather than the fine alligatoring you see in more humid regions. That cracking pattern can look cosmetic from the ground, but it's often shedding paint chips directly into flower beds and foundation soil, which matters if small children or pets are on the property. We flag it during testing even when it wasn't the reason for the original service call.
