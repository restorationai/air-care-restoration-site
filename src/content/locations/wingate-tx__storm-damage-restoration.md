**Storm damage emergency in Wingate?** We answer 24/7 and send a crew out to assess roof damage, downed trees, and water intrusion before a hard rain turns a cracked shingle into a soaked ceiling. Wingate's flat, exposed terrain and the severe thunderstorms that roll through this stretch of the Big Country mean storm damage here rarely stays contained to one spot on the property, and the sooner a tarp or board goes up, the less it costs to fix.

## Why Wingate Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Wingate sits in open ranch country west of Abilene, and that exposure works against roofs and trees during spring and early summer storm season. Straight-line winds and hail moving across unobstructed farmland hit metal roofing and older shingle roofs hard, and mesquite and pecan trees with shallow root systems come down easily in saturated clay soil after a heavy rain. A lot of the housing stock out here is older pier-and-beam construction or manufactured homes, both of which handle wind uplift and water intrusion differently than a slab-built home in town. Once wind peels back roofing material or a tree limb punches through a roof deck, water finds its way into attic insulation and ceiling joists fast, and in the Big Country's heat and humidity swings, that trapped moisture can start supporting mold growth within a day or two if it isn't dried out properly.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Wingate

The first call is about stopping further damage, not starting repairs. We tarp exposed roof sections, board up broken windows or damaged siding, and remove downed limbs or fallen trees that are blocking access or still resting against the structure. From there we extract any standing water, pull wet insulation and drywall that won't dry in place, and set up air movers and dehumidifiers sized for the space, following IICRC S500 drying guidelines so moisture readings in framing and subfloor actually get back to normal rather than just looking dry on the surface. For pier-and-beam homes common in and around Wingate, that often means getting equipment under the house to dry the crawlspace, not just the living area above it. Every step gets photographed and documented for your insurance claim as we go.

A lot of Wingate-area homes still rely on well water and septic systems, and a downed tree or storm surge of runoff can knock out a well pump or damage a septic line in addition to the structural damage up top. We flag that during the initial walk-through because a homeowner focused on a hole in the roof can easily miss a well or septic issue until the water stops running a day later.
