## Why Stamford Properties See Water Damage Issues

Stamford sits on soil with a high clay content typical of Jones County, and that soil expands when it's wet and shrinks hard when it dries out. That cycle puts constant stress on slab foundations, and over years it opens hairline cracks that let plumbing lines underneath shift and eventually fail. A slow leak under a slab can run for weeks before anyone notices a warm spot on the floor or a spike in the water bill, and by then the moisture has often traveled well past the original crack. Add in the area's pattern of intense, short-duration spring and summer storms, the kind that dump a lot of rain fast on ground that can't absorb it quickly, and older homes with low-set additions or crawlspaces end up taking on water through foundation vents and door thresholds even when the roof never leaked at all.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Stamford

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted and portable units before it has a chance to wick further into drywall, baseboards, and subflooring. From there we map moisture with meters and thermal imaging, because in a slab-foundation home the wet area under the flooring is almost always larger than what's visible at the surface. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and adjusted based on daily readings rather than a fixed schedule, since concrete slabs here release trapped moisture more slowly than a wood-framed subfloor would. We document every stage with photos and moisture logs, which matters both for drying accuracy and for the insurance claim that usually follows.
