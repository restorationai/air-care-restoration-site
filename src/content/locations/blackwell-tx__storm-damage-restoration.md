## Why Blackwell Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Blackwell sits in a stretch of West Texas where spring and early summer bring fast-moving thunderstorm cells, straight-line winds, and the occasional tornado spinning off a supercell. Homes and outbuildings here are often older frame construction or metal-roofed structures built for a dry climate, not necessarily for sustained hail impact or the kind of wind gusts that come with a severe thunderstorm watch. Caliche-heavy soil in the area also means poor drainage after a heavy rain event, so a damaged roof or broken window doesn't just mean water coming in overhead, it means standing water pooling against foundations faster than the ground can absorb it. Metal roofing common on rural properties and outbuildings can hide hail damage until a heavier rain finds the dented seams.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Blackwell

We start with a structural safety check, because downed power lines and compromised roof decking are common after a wind event out here and we're not walking a homeowner back into a property until it's safe. From there we tarp exposed roof sections and board up broken windows or doors to stop secondary water and pest intrusion, then move into water extraction if rain has already gotten into the structure. Tree limbs on older mesquite and live oak often take gutters and fascia boards down with them, so we document that damage separately from wind or hail damage since carriers sometimes categorize them differently. Drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, and we track moisture readings in framing and subfloor until materials are back to a stable baseline rather than just surface-dry.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Storm Damage

West Texas storms tend to hit hard and move on, which means the damage is often concentrated but severe: a section of roof deck exposed to open sky, a window blown in with glass and debris scattered across flooring. We bring commercial dehumidifiers and air movers sized for the structure, moisture meters to check behind drywall and under flooring where wind-driven rain can travel further than it looks, and tarping or board-up materials rated to hold through follow-up storms while permanent repairs are scheduled. For properties with asbestos-era roofing or siding materials, which still turn up in older Blackwell-area buildings, we handle testing and abatement in coordination with the repair work rather than disturbing those materials blind.

## Blackwell Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most storm claims out here involve wind and hail as a combined peril, and adjusters want clear photo documentation of the damage before any tarping or debris removal happens, which is why we photograph everything as a first step, not an afterthought. Rural and small-town properties in this part of Texas don't typically carry HOA restrictions, but outbuildings, fencing, and detached garages often get missed in an initial claim estimate, so we make a point of walking the full property rather than just the main structure when we scope the loss.

## Local note

One thing that catches people off guard in this part of West Texas: a roof that looks fine from the ground after a hail event can still have granule loss and bruised shingles that won't show a leak until weeks later during the next heavy rain. We check attic decking and valleys for early signs of impact damage even when the homeowner's main concern is the downed tree limb or broken window that prompted the call, because the hail damage is often what the insurance claim ultimately hinges on.
